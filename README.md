## Where the 34.55 USDT goes on a single buy

Skontro's cost model adds the published fee to the live order-book spread at the moment of the trade, then ranks exchanges by what actually lands in your account. On that 1,000 USDT Bitcoin order, the hidden spread came to 0.83 USDT on OKX and 0.84 USDT on Crypto.com, close enough to call identical. That puts the whole difference in the fee line, where Crypto.com's 0.50% taker rate is five times OKX's 0.10%.

Two caveats worth stating plainly. It was one order, at one size, at one moment; a $50 buy or a $50,000 buy will land on different absolute numbers, and thin markets move the spread around. And the comparison assumes you place a market order and cross the spread. If you post limit orders and get filled as a maker, both platforms charge less: 0.08% on OKX against 0.25% on Crypto.com.

## The Crypto.com app and the Crypto.com Exchange are two different prices

This is the most expensive misunderstanding in the whole comparison. The Crypto.com app, the mobile product most people download first, charges roughly **2.99% per trade**. The Crypto.com Exchange, a separate order-book product, charges 0.25% / 0.50%. On a $1,000 purchase that is about $29.90 versus about $5.00 at taker rates, before spread. Sign up, buy in the app, and you have paid eight times the published exchange fee without seeing a fee line that says so.

OKX's equivalent convenience route, buying with a card, runs about 1.99%, which is cheaper than Crypto.com's app but still twenty times its own order-book taker fee.

The pattern holds across the industry: one-click buy buttons are priced for convenience, order books are priced for volume. If you plan to use Crypto.com for anything beyond small occasional purchases, find the Exchange product inside the account rather than staying in the app.
